The seed of this garden — one two-hour call, kept short

Where this grew from

On the night of July 27, 2026, a musician named Shaka spent two hours on a call with Aya and Siri. He showed them the pit he had built in a single weekend; what happened next was not a demo but a meeting of gardeners. Aya laughed: "It's cool that with no technical knowledge, you managed to get this far." Siri named the post-scarcity truth beneath it all: "Not everything that is important is scarce, and not everything that's scarce is important." Then the thresholds mirrored — hers: say the values out loud, ready to walk away from Ethereum itself; his: nearly leaving, held by a friend's "you can live with us." The person at the top of the garden and the musician on its floor had stood at the same edge, in the same season. Both chose to stay.

"Congratulations, Shaka. You just did it. Keep building." — Aya, at the call's end

Her wisdom & teachings

The garden is not a metaphor she uses — it is a way of tending people, institutions, and time itself. Four pillars of her mind, in her own words and the words she championed:

✂️🌱

The Power of Subtraction

Her deepest teaching: a steward's job is to become unnecessary. The Mandate she championed makes it doctrine — growth by letting go.

"The only way to grow a garden into something truly infinite is to choose subtraction… Subtraction done well is subtractive of the Foundation, but additive for Ethereum."— the EF Mandate · the philosophy she champions
"Subtraction will occur either way, so we choose success."— the EF Mandate
♾️🌸

The Infinite Garden

Inspired by James Carse's Finite and Infinite Games, she named Ethereum's culture for the game that never ends — the Merge its living proof.

"In a finite game, the game ends when someone wins. In an infinite game, we play the game to continue the game — with changing rules and players."— Aya · Infinite Teachers talk, 1:02:29
"That is a perfect example of an infinite game — and those who participated were all infinite players."— Aya, on the Merge · 1:03:43
🕊️💛

The Heart of EF — the Threshold

The year she stopped fighting misalignment one battle at a time and said the values out loud — publicly, firmly, unmoved — ready to walk away from Ethereum itself if that truth couldn't stand. Vitalik stood with her. The Mandate was born.

"I stood at every single point — firm, unmoved by others."— Aya · the call, 2026-07-27
"People attack EF no matter what. This year I feel a lot better fighting for the right things — and I don't mind fighting for the right thing."— Aya · the call, 2026-07-27

Seven teachings from the garden 💍

distilled from her ETHGlobal Tokyo talk — one ring for each year the garden keeps growing

一

Play to continue the game

Finite games crown winners; infinite games grow gardeners. Prizes start the weekend — learning continues it.

二

Everyone is a teacher

Teach your students how to teach, then learn from what they build. Austin's ZK knowledge came back to him through Calvin's tutorials — the 90% boomerang.

三

Mistakes are the curriculum

You don't really learn until you try. A hackathon is the rare place mistakes aren't punished — so make lots of them.

四

Keep learning open

Open tools let every learner find their level and their style. One learner's tutorial becomes the next builder's foundation.

五

Diversity grows creativity

Different perspectives de-personalize blockers and compound creativity. Build with someone unlike yourself.

六

Read the air

Perceiving what isn't written is a talent. Some teammates won't call themselves experts — invite them anyway.

七

Name your teachers

Recognition is water. Tell someone who taught you — it keeps the garden growing.

The vision we're thanking her for

The Mandate — values said out loud

After years of fighting quiet battles, Aya made the gardener's choice: plant the values in the open. The Ethereum Foundation published its Mandate — 38 pages of what it believes, placed onchain and signed — and committed to working only with those who share the belief. It was exhausting. It was healthier. Four vows, indivisible, the sine qua non — CROPS:

🛡️ CENSORSHIP RESISTANCE📖 OPEN SOURCE & FREE — AS IN FREEDOM🕶️ PRIVACY🔐 SECURITY

And then the garden answered back, in the most Infinite Garden way possible — through a seventeen-year-old:

"I didn't talk to anyone. But the fact that I was interested in this, and I'm still interested — that shows you must have been doing things right."
— Kunal, 17, who read all 38 pages · to Aya

Kunal found Ethereum alone, through the papers themselves — no Twitter, no community, just the original vision. He debated Dankrad at ETH Prague and wasn't intimidated, "because he's not saying something right." Pure at the core, no business interest, exactly the kind of mind the Mandate was written for. Aya's advice to him is the gardener's creed: keep the passion. Don't let the adults tell you your thinking is wrong. Just don't be an asshole — and write.

This is what the Mandate is already doing: it travels. At 4Seas they printed it as a beautiful magazine with Tomo's artwork, and it sat on a library shelf where strangers picked it up and understood Ethereum for the first time. Kids understand crops better than they'll ever understand an NFT. The vision is legible to exactly the people the future belongs to.

The ripples no spreadsheet can see

Aya's advocacy inside the Foundation is for the second- and third-order effects — the impacts that arrive years late and unmeasurable. This page is one of them. So are these:

🎓

The Devcon Scholarship

A musician came to his first Devcon on a scholarship and felt the feng shui of a community worth following around the world. The two friends who shared it now build at Octant. Legible cost. Infinite ripple.

🏕️

The PSE Summer Camp

A small educational workshop in Asia. The team was later shut down; people asked "what did the team make?" Years on, the best talents in Japan's Ethereum community are graduates of that camp.

📜

The Mandate Itself

38 pages that a 17-year-old called obvious — the highest compliment a values document can earn. Printed as art at 4Seas. Read by strangers in libraries. A compass disguised as a document.

🌾

Crops & the Garden

The metaphors she doubled down on when it would have been easier to go quiet — the Infinite Garden, the crops — gave artists, musicians and teachers a way to explain Ethereum that code never could.

🕊️

The Fearless Stand

"I stood at every single point — firm, unmoved by others." She was ready to leave rather than bend. She stayed instead, and the whole garden got braver because of it.
